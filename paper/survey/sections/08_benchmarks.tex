\section{Benchmarks and Reported Results}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

This section collects the reported results of the 112 methods that the systematic search included. The tables
list numbers as each paper prints them. They are not a controlled comparison, and the prose below
says where a ranking is meaningful and where it is not.

\subsection{Protocol and comparability}
\label{sec:bench-protocol}

Almost every included paper uses the CIFAR-100 protocol of CRD \citep{tian2020crd}, as packaged in
DKD's \texttt{mdistiller} code base \citep{zhao2022dkd}: pre-trained teachers released with CRD,
240 training epochs, SGD, and top-1 accuracy on the 10k validation images
(\S\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}). The pair reported most often is ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4,
on which classical KD reaches 73.33\% \citep[Table~6]{zhao2022dkd}; we rank by it. WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2
is the second homogeneous pair, and it is the only pair most data-free papers report. On ImageNet-1K
the standard pairs are ResNet34$\to$ResNet18 and ResNet50$\to$MobileNetV1.

Three things limit cross-paper comparison even under this protocol. First, papers report
different statistics: some give the mean of three or five runs, some the best run, and a few a single
run (for example RDD, \citealp{mugisha2026rdd}). Gaps under about 0.3 points on CIFAR-100 are within the
seed spread most papers report and should not be read as a ranking. Second, many top numbers are
\emph{stacked}: a plug-in is added on top of an already strong method such as MLKD
\citep{jin2023mlkd}, CRLD or SimKD \citep{chen2022simkd}, so the row measures the combination rather
than the new idea alone. The Flags column names the base method and, where the paper gives it,
the stand-alone number. Third, some rows change the setting itself: a retrained or updated teacher
(KD-AIF, \citealp{wu2025kdaif}; DJIP, \citealp{chen2026djip}; BTKD, \citealp{zhang2025btkd}), extra
data or augmentation (KD$^3$, \citealp{tang2023kd3}; SRD, \citealp{miles2024projector}), label
smoothing on the student (LSKD and EA-KD rows), online or multi-student training (ORC,
\citealp{choi2023orc}; ADM, \citealp{chen2025adm}; MC-Distil, \citealp{tambat2025mcdistil}), a searched
distiller (KD-Zero, \citealp{li2023kdzero}; Auto-KD, \citealp{li2023autokd}), or a stronger KD baseline
than \texttt{mdistiller}'s, as in SKD \citep{jeong2026skd} (KD 74.75) and DiSK \citep{kag2023disk} (KD 76.25).
Those rows are kept and flagged rather than dropped, following the search rulings.

\subsection{CIFAR-100}
\label{sec:bench-cifar}

Tables~\ref{tab:bench-cifar-a} and~\ref{tab:bench-cifar-b} list the 72 methods with a CIFAR-100
number on a standard homogeneous pair. KD-AIF's 82.20\% is listed but not ranked, because its teacher
is updated to 86.56\%. The five highest reported configurations among the rest are DeepKD at 79.25\% \citep{huang2025deepkd},
VRM at 78.76\% \citep{zhang2025vrm}, EA-KD at 78.38\% \citep{su2025eakd}, SKD at 78.33\%
\citep{jeong2026skd} and LSKD at 78.28\% \citep{sun2024lskd}. Only VRM and SKD are single methods
here: DeepKD is stacked on CRLD, EA-KD's best row is EA-MLD with label smoothing (EA-KD alone gives
75.46\%), and LSKD's is MLKD with logit standardisation. Among single methods with an official
repository, the best are SimKD at 78.08\% \citep{chen2022simkd}, GDKD at 77.76\%
\citep{zheng2025gdkd} and LumiNet at 77.50\% \citep{hossain2025luminet}. The WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2
column is sparse because the search files recorded it only where no ResNet32$\times$4 number existed;
DistPro \citep{deng2022distpro} reports 76.36\% there.

\subsection{ImageNet-1K}
\label{sec:bench-imagenet}

Tables~\ref{tab:bench-imagenet-a} and~\ref{tab:bench-imagenet-b} list 79 methods on
ResNet34$\to$ResNet18. The spread is narrow: all but seven rows fall between 71.0\% and 73.0\%, against
71.70\% for DKD. The highest values come with a caveat. DeepKD's 74.23\% is again stacked on CRLD.
The topology method TopoKD reports 73.60\% \citep{kim2024topkd}, above the 73.31\% of its own teacher, and
BTKD and its journal version report 73.38\% with their own teachers and optimiser
\citep{zhang2025btkd,zhang2026btkdpp}. Some ImageNet rows also differ in setting: the MSE-teacher study trains for 120 epochs \citep{hamidi2024mseteacher}, and
CheckpointKD uses its own 73.86\% teacher \citep{wang2022checkpointkd}. Only three included papers
were read for ResNet50$\to$MobileNetV1 in this pass: MCD 73.65\% \citep{mishra2026mcd}, DIST+ 73.47\%
\citep{huang2025distplus} and LB 73.25\% \citep{lin2026lb}.

\subsection{Comparison across families}
\label{sec:bench-families}

\paragraph{Logit methods.} Logit methods are the most numerous group in the tables and the cheapest
to run: most add one loss on the teacher's outputs and need no projector. Most single logit methods fall
between 76.0\% (WTTM) and 77.8\% (GDKD), and the stacked rows above 78\% mostly combine two of
them. Their weakness is that the gains shrink on ImageNet, where the same methods sit within about one
point of DKD. They apply to any pair with a shared label space, including heterogeneous ones.

\paragraph{Feature and relation methods.} VRM, NCKD and SimKD give the highest single-method CIFAR
numbers, but SimKD reuses the teacher's classifier, so the deployed student is larger than ResNet8$\times$4
alone \citep{chen2022simkd}, and VRM has no public code. Feature methods need a projector or alignment
module per pair and access to intermediate layers, which makes them harder to move to new
architectures. Several recent ones (OFA-KD, HCD, UHKD, CoCaRS) target heterogeneous pairs and report only
on ImageNet.

\paragraph{Training schemes and searched distillers.} Online and multi-student schemes (ORC, ADM,
SwitOKD, MC-Distil) score lower on ResNet8$\times$4 than offline methods, but they solve a different
problem: they avoid or reduce the cost of a fixed pre-trained teacher. Searched distillers (KD-Zero,
Auto-KD, UniADS) reach 76.6--77.9\% at the price of a search per pair.

\paragraph{Reliability methods.} Methods that decide which teacher outputs to trust (RLD, IPWD, TLLM)
or that retrain the teacher for the student (DJIP, MCMI, SoTeacher) give 75.5--76.6\% as single methods on ResNet8$\times$4 where they report it.
The teacher-side ones are hard to compare because each changes the teacher.

\paragraph{Optimizer-side methods.} Four included methods change how the task and distillation
gradients are combined rather than what is distilled. DOT keeps separate momentum for the two
gradients and reports KD+DOT at 75.12\%, a 1.8-point gain over KD \citep{zhao2023dot}. DeepKD extends
that idea with per-component momentum and top-$k$ denoising and reaches 79.25\% when stacked on CRLD
\citep{huang2025deepkd}. DTO-KD sets the trade-off from a min-norm combination of the two gradients and
reports 76.40\% \citep{hayder2026dtokd}. DistPro meta-learns weights over distillation pathways
\citep{deng2022distpro}. None of them gates the distillation term per sample; reliability methods do
that on the loss side. Priority-constrained descent (PCD)
\citep{varam2026pcd} belongs to the same family of gradient rules but has no reported number on these pairs, so it is not in the tables.
Their numbers also show that an optimizer change can be stacked on a loss change, so a fair test
of an optimiser should report it on top of both KD and a strong logit loss.

\paragraph{Applicability.} For a new pair with no intermediate-layer access, a logit method with
public code (DKD, MLKD, LSKD, WKD, LumiNet) is the safe starting point. When the student's layers are
accessible and the pair is homogeneous, SimKD or a projector-based feature method is the stronger choice on the reported CIFAR-100 numbers. When the teacher may be wrong on part of the data, the reliability methods are
the relevant group, and when the cost of a pre-trained teacher is the constraint, the online schemes or
the data-free methods are.
