\section{Benchmarks and Reported Results}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

The numbers below are the results each included paper reports for itself, so they do not form a controlled comparison. Supplementary Table~S2 lists all 112 included methods with their results; the tables here keep the top results and the baselines discussed in the text.

\subsection{Protocol and comparability}
\label{sec:bench-protocol}

Almost every included paper uses the CIFAR-100 protocol of CRD \citep{tian2020crd}, as packaged in
DKD's \texttt{mdistiller} code base \citep{zhao2022dkd}: CRD's pre-trained teachers, 240 epochs, SGD, and
top-1 accuracy on the 10k validation images (\S\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}). We rank by the most reported
pair, ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4, where classical KD reaches 73.33\% \citep[Table~6]{zhao2022dkd}.
WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2 is the second homogeneous pair and the only one most data-free papers report. On
ImageNet-1K the standard pairs are ResNet34$\to$ResNet18 and ResNet50$\to$MobileNetV1.

Three things limit cross-paper comparison even under this protocol. First, papers report different
statistics: the mean of three or five runs, the best run, or a single run.
Gaps under about 0.3 points on CIFAR-100 are within the usual seed spread and should not be read as a
ranking. Second, many top numbers are \emph{stacked}: a plug-in is added to an already strong method such
as MLKD \citep{jin2023mlkd}, SimKD \citep{chen2022simkd} or CRLD, the base of DeepKD's best result \citep[Table~1]{huang2025deepkd}, so the number measures the combination. The
Flags column names the base and, where given, the stand-alone number. Third, some results come from a changed setting:
a retrained or updated teacher (KD-AIF, \citealp{wu2025kdaif}), extra data or augmentation (SRD,
\citealp{miles2024projector}), label smoothing on the student (LSKD and EA-KD), online or
multi-student training, a searched distiller, or a stronger KD baseline than \texttt{mdistiller}'s, as in SKD
\citep{jeong2026skd} (KD 74.75). Following our screening rules,
these results are kept and flagged.

\subsection{CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K}
\label{sec:bench-cifar}
\label{sec:bench-imagenet}

Seventy-two methods report a CIFAR-100 number on a standard homogeneous pair (Table~\ref{tab:bench-cifar}).
KD-AIF's 82.20\% is listed but not ranked, because its teacher is updated to 86.56\%. The five highest
configurations among the rest are DeepKD at 79.25\% \citep{huang2025deepkd}, VRM at 78.76\%
\citep{zhang2025vrm}, EA-KD at 78.38\% \citep{su2025eakd}, SKD at 78.33\% \citep{jeong2026skd} and LSKD at
78.28\% \citep{sun2024lskd}. Only VRM and SKD are single methods here: DeepKD is stacked on CRLD, EA-KD's best
result comes from EA-MLD with label smoothing (EA-KD alone gives 75.46\%), and LSKD's is MLKD with logit
standardisation. Among single methods with an official repository, the best are SimKD at 78.08\%
\citep{chen2022simkd}, GDKD at 77.76\% \citep{zheng2025gdkd} and LumiNet at 77.50\% \citep{hossain2025luminet}.
Few methods report WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2; among them, DistPro reaches 76.36\% \citep{deng2022distpro} and DeepKD 78.42\%.

On ImageNet ResNet34$\to$ResNet18 (79 methods, Table~\ref{tab:bench-imagenet}) the spread is narrow: all but
seven of them fall between 71.0\% and 73.0\%, against 71.70\% for DKD. The highest values carry caveats. TopoKD
reports 73.60\% \citep{kim2024topkd}, above the 73.31\% of its own teacher. Only three included papers (plus DeepKD, re-read from the camera-ready version)
were read for ResNet50$\to$MobileNetV1: MCD 73.65\% \citep{mishra2026mcd}, DIST+ 73.47\%
\citep{huang2025distplus} and LB 73.25\% \citep{lin2026lb} (last column of Table~\ref{tab:bench-imagenet}). DeepKD stacked on CRLD reports 73.34\% and
74.85\% on the two ImageNet pairs.

\subsection{Data-free and untabulated methods}
\label{sec:bench-datafree}

Ten data-free methods (\S\ref{sec:schemes}) report WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2 values under their own synthesis budgets, and four more methods meet the criteria only on a pair not tabulated here; all are in supplementary Table~S2 and not ranked.

\subsection{Comparison across families}
\label{sec:bench-families}

\paragraph{Logit methods.} These are the most numerous and the cheapest methods. Most single logit methods fall between 76.0\% and 77.8\% (GDKD), the stacked results above 78\% mostly combine two of them, and on ImageNet they sit within about one point of DKD.

\paragraph{Feature and relation methods.} VRM and SimKD give the highest single-method CIFAR numbers, but
SimKD reuses the teacher's classifier, so the deployed student is larger than ResNet8$\times$4 alone
\citep{chen2022simkd}, and VRM has no public code. Feature methods need a projector per pair and intermediate-layer access; several recent ones report only on ImageNet.

\paragraph{Training schemes and reliability.} Online and multi-student schemes
score lower than offline methods but avoid a fixed pre-trained teacher; searched distillers reach 76.6--77.9\% at the price of a search per pair. Reliability methods such as RLD and the teacher-retraining ones give 75.5--76.6\% as single methods where they report it.

\paragraph{Optimizer-side methods.} DOT (KD+DOT 75.12\%, \citealp{zhao2023dot}), DeepKD (79.25\% stacked on CRLD, \citealp{huang2025deepkd}), DTO-KD (76.40\%, \citealp{hayder2026dtokd}) and DistPro \citep{deng2022distpro} change how the task and distillation gradients are
combined (\S\ref{sec:kd_moo}); none gates the distillation term per sample. PCD \citep{varam2026pcd} has no reported number on these pairs.

\paragraph{A comparison set for combination rules.} For a method that changes how the task and distillation terms are combined, the tables suggest three groups of baselines. Combination rules: a tuned weighted sum \citep{hinton2015kd}, MGDA \citep{sener2018mgda}, PCGrad \citep{yu2020pcgrad}, DOT \citep{zhao2023dot} and DTO-KD \citep{hayder2026dtokd}, with DeepKD \citep{huang2025deepkd} quoted from its paper since no code is linked. Loss-side methods with code: DKD, MLKD, LSKD (MLKD+LS) and GDKD, plus SimKD where the student can keep the teacher's classifier. Reliability methods that act on a wrong teacher: RLD and MCD. Since an optimiser change stacks on a loss change, each combination rule should be run on top of both KD and a strong logit loss. VRM and SKD link no code, so they are quoted with the protocol caveats above.

\paragraph{Applicability.} Without intermediate-layer access, a logit method with public code
(DKD, MLKD, LSKD, LumiNet) is the safe start; for accessible homogeneous pairs, SimKD or a projector-based feature method is stronger on the reported numbers; reliability methods fit a partly wrong teacher, and online or data-free schemes fit when a pre-trained teacher is the constraint.
