\section{Logit-Based Distillation}
\label{sec:logit}

Logit-based methods supervise the student through the teacher's output layer; in their output-only form they need no projector and fit any pair of classifiers with the same label space. Until 2022 they trailed feature methods: on ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4, classical KD \citep{hinton2015kd} reaches 73.33\% against 75.51\% for CRD and 75.63\% for ReviewKD \citep[Table~6]{zhao2022dkd}.
% src: DKD Table 6 (KD 73.33, CRD 75.51, ReviewKD 75.63)
Later work closed the gap by changing what the KL term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kd-objective} measures. We group methods by how they decompose the loss (\S\ref{sec:logit-decouple}), rescale the logits (\S\ref{sec:logit-scale}), or replace the divergence (\S\ref{sec:logit-div}); each method's own number on the common pair is in \S\ref{sec:benchmarks} or in supplementary Table~S2.

\subsection{Decoupling the KD loss}
\label{sec:logit-decouple}

DKD \citep{zhao2022dkd} splits KL into a binary target-class term (TCKD) and a non-target term (NCKD). In classical KD the non-target term is scaled by $1-p^{T}_{y}(\mathbf{z}_t)$ and so vanishes on the confidently predicted samples that DKD's ablation finds most useful; DKD removes this coupling with separate weights, $\alpha\,\mathrm{TCKD}+\beta\,\mathrm{NCKD}$, and lifts ResNet8$\times$4 from 73.33\% to 76.32\%, above CRD and ReviewKD at the cost of plain KD \citep[Table~6]{zhao2022dkd}. Its code base (\texttt{mdistiller}) underlies most later logit work. The authors state its limits: $\beta$ must be tuned per pair, and a logit-only loss cannot transfer the localisation knowledge detectors need, so DKD trails ReviewKD on COCO.
% src: DKD Table 6 (DKD 76.32, KD 73.33)
Several later methods normalise the non-target part (NKD, \citealp{yang2023nkd}) or distil relations between local class pairs; their gains over DKD are mostly within the cross-paper spread of \S\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}. LDRLD \citep{xu2025ldrld}, the dense local-relation variant, reports 77.20\% \citep[Table~1]{xu2025ldrld}, at a cost that grows with the number of pairs.
% src: LDRLD Table 1 (77.20)
GDKD \citep{zheng2025gdkd} generalises the target/non-target split into a partition between the teacher's top classes and the rest, reporting 77.76\% against 76.64\% for its own DKD run \citep[Table~I]{zheng2025gdkd}, a baseline 0.32 points above DKD's figure.
% src: GDKD T I (77.76; own DKD 76.64). GDKD: CORE-P preprint.

\subsection{Rescaling the logits: temperature and standardisation}
\label{sec:logit-scale}

CTKD \citep{li2023ctkd} learns the temperature adversarially through a gradient-reversal layer on a curriculum; gains are modest but consistent (ImageNet KD 70.83\%$\to$71.32\%, DKD 71.13\%$\to$71.51\%, \citealp[Table~4]{li2023ctkd}), and LSKD's reproduction gives KD+CTKD 73.39\% on our pair \citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd}. LSKD \citep{sun2024lskd} argues that a small student need not reach the teacher's logit range, since only relations between logits matter, and z-score standardises each logit vector before the softmax, a per-sample temperature. As a plug-in it lifts KD from 73.33\% to 76.62\%, DKD to 77.01\% and MLKD from 77.08\% to 78.28\% \citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd}, so standardisation and decoupling combine; gains shrink on other pairs (0.13 points for DKD on VGG13$\to$VGG8) and LDRLD reproduces KD+LS at 76.16\% \citep[Table~1]{xu2025ldrld}.
% src: CTKD Table 4; LSKD Table 2 (73.39, 76.62, 77.01, 78.28, +0.13); LDRLD Table 1 (76.16)
LumiNet \citep{hossain2025luminet} normalises each class logit with batch statistics instead, reporting 77.50\% over five runs and an expected calibration error of 0.06 against 0.11 for KD \citep[Tables~1 and~5]{hossain2025luminet}; its targets therefore depend on batch composition.
% src: LumiNet T1/T5.

\subsection{Replacing or enriching the KL divergence}
\label{sec:logit-div}

DIST \citep{huang2022dist} targets the capacity gap by replacing KL with inter- and intra-class Pearson correlation, which keeps the ranking of predictions while ignoring shift and scale (76.31\%, ImageNet 72.07\%, \citealp[Tables~5 and~2]{huang2022dist}); it is weaker for binary tasks. MLKD \citep{jin2023mlkd} aligns predictions at instance, batch and class level and augments them over several temperatures, reporting 77.08\% \citep[Table~2]{jin2023mlkd}; part of its gain may come from the temperature augmentation. WKD replaces KL with a Wasserstein distance that uses relations between classes \citep{lv2024wkd}.
% src: DIST T5/T2; MLKD T2

Batch and sample structure is the last axis. SKD \citep{jeong2026skd} adds batch Gram-matrix alignment to instance KL and reports 78.33\%, the highest single-method logit number we found, though its KD baseline of 74.75\% is about 1.4 points above the usual 73.33\% \citep[Table~1]{jeong2026skd}. EA-KD \citep{su2025eakd} weights samples by entropy, 75.46\% alone and 78.38\% stacked as EA-MLD+LS \citep[Table~2]{su2025eakd}. RLD \citep{sun2025rld} uses the label to mask every class the teacher ranks at or above the true class, so a wrong teacher ranking is not transferred (76.64\%, 0.32 above DKD, \citealp[Table~1]{sun2025rld}); it and EA-KD are analysed further in \S\ref{sec:reliability}.
% src: SKD T1 (78.33; KD 74.75); EA-KD T2 (75.46; 78.38); RLD T1 (76.64; DKD 76.32)

\subsection{Comparison and outlook}
\label{sec:logit-compare}

% INTERPRETATION below unless cited.
Since LSKD wraps several bases \citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd}, decomposition, rescaling and divergence behave as separable axes. The 2024--2025 methods report 76.0--77.5\%, a range comparable to cross-paper reproduction spread (\S\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}), so we name no single best method; only the step of about three points from KD to the 2022 methods is consistent across sources. DKD and KD+LSKD are cheap re-reported baselines with public code \citep[Table~1]{xu2025ldrld}; the highest values (MLKD+LSKD, SKD, EA-MLD+LS) come with extra terms, a stronger baseline, or stacking. Recent work shifts to which teacher signal to trust (RLD; \S\ref{sec:reliability}), class imbalance, and balancing the KD term against the task loss (\S\ref{sec:kd_moo}). \S\ref{sec:benchmarks} and supplementary Table~S2 give the full included set.
