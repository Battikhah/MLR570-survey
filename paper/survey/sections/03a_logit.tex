\section{Logit-Based Distillation}
\label{sec:logit}

Logit-based methods supervise the student through the teacher's output layer. In their conventional output-only form, classical KD and most of the methods below, they need no projector between feature maps of different shapes, cost little more than training the student alone, and apply to any pair of classifiers with the same label space. Two methods in this section relax that: SDD computes local logits from the final feature maps, and WKD-L builds its cost matrix from feature-space category relations, so both read intermediate features and add computation while keeping a logit-level loss. Until 2022 they were
also thought to trail feature-based methods by a clear margin. On ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4,
classical KD \citep{hinton2015kd} reaches 73.33\% against 75.51\% for CRD and 75.63\% for ReviewKD
\citep[Table~6]{zhao2022dkd}.
% src: DKD Table 6 (KD 73.33, CRD 75.51, ReviewKD 75.63)
Work since 2022 has closed that gap mainly by changing what the KL term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:kd-objective} measures, with feature-matching losses left out. We group the methods by the part of the objective they change:
how the loss is decomposed (\S\ref{sec:logit-decouple}), how the logits are scaled before the softmax
(\S\ref{sec:logit-scale}), and which discrepancy replaces the KL divergence
(\S\ref{sec:logit-div}). Table~\ref{tab:logit-sota} lists each method with its own reported number
on the common pair. Methods that change \emph{which} teacher outputs to trust, such as RLD, are
introduced here and analysed in \S\ref{sec:reliability}.

\subsection{Decoupling the KD loss}
\label{sec:logit-decouple}

\paragraph{DKD.} \citet{zhao2022dkd} rewrite the KL term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kd-objective} as the sum of
a binary target-class term (TCKD), which compares the probability each network puts on the true class,
and a non-target term (NCKD), which compares the distributions over the remaining $C-1$ classes. In
classical KD the non-target term is multiplied by $1-p^{T}_{y}(\mathbf{z}_t)$, so it shrinks exactly
on the samples the teacher predicts confidently, which DKD's ablation finds to be the most useful
ones. DKD removes this coupling and weights the two terms with separate hyper-parameters,
$\alpha\,\mathrm{TCKD}+\beta\,\mathrm{NCKD}$. This lifts ResNet8$\times$4 from 73.33\% to 76.32\%
\citep[Table~6]{zhao2022dkd}, a result above CRD and ReviewKD on that pair at the training cost of
plain KD.
% src: DKD Table 6 (DKD 76.32, KD 73.33)
The decomposition has become the reference point for later logit methods, and its code base
(\texttt{mdistiller}) is the one most of them build on. Its weaknesses are stated by the authors. The
best $\beta$ depends on the teacher--student pair and must be tuned, and a logit-only loss cannot pass
on the localisation knowledge that detectors need, so DKD trails ReviewKD on COCO
\citep{zhao2022dkd}. DKD suits settings where a feature-free, low-cost baseline is
needed and some tuning of $\beta$ is acceptable.

\paragraph{NKD.} \citet{yang2023nkd} reach a similar diagnosis from a different direction. They split
KD into a target loss and a non-target loss and note that the teacher's non-target probabilities
sum to $1-p_y$, which varies between samples and so changes the scale of the non-target term. NKD
normalises the non-target distributions of teacher and student before matching them. The same paper
derives USKD, a teacher-free variant with customised soft labels (\S\ref{sec:schemes}). NKD reports
76.35\% on ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4 and 71.96\% on ImageNet ResNet34$\to$ResNet18
\citep[Table~1]{yang2023nkd}, which is level with DKD on CIFAR-100 (76.32\% in the same table).
% src: NKD Table 1 (NKD 76.35 / 71.96; DKD 76.32 / 71.70)
Compared with DKD, NKD replaces a tuned weight $\beta$ with a fixed normalisation, so it has fewer
hyper-parameters to search; the price is that it cannot up-weight the non-target term when a pair
would benefit from it.
% INTERPRETATION
The 0.03-point difference on CIFAR-100 is probably far smaller than the cross-paper spread
noted in \S\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}.

\paragraph{SDD.} \citet{wei2024sdd} decouple along space instead of along classes. They pool the
teacher's and student's final feature maps at several scales, pass each pooled region through the
classifier to obtain local logits, and split the local terms into \emph{consistent} ones (local and
global prediction agree) and \emph{complementary} ones (they disagree), giving the latter a larger
weight. The decomposition is a plug-in for KD, DKD and NKD. Their ablation reports 76.63\% for SD-KD
on our reference pair against 73.33\% for KD \citep[Table~6]{wei2024sdd}; on ImageNet SD-NKD reaches
72.33\% and SD-DKD 72.02\% \citep[Table~3]{wei2024sdd}.
% src: SDD Table 6 (Fusion 76.63 vs N/A 73.33, ResNet32x4-ResNet8x4); SDD Table 3 (ImageNet)
SDD reads more of the network than the other logit methods, because the local logits need the last
feature map, but it still needs no projector. Other groups did not reproduce its CIFAR-100 gain at
the same level: \citet{xiang2025ekd} obtain 75.92\% for SDD and \citet{xu2025ldrld} 75.09\% on the
same pair.
% src: EKD Table 1 (SDD 75.92*, own reproduction); LDRLD Table 1 (SDD 75.09)
SDD is most useful for fine-grained classes and heterogeneous pairs, where the authors report that
the multi-scale terms matter most \citep{wei2024sdd}.

\paragraph{LDRLD.} \citet{xu2025ldrld} decouple at the finest granularity. Instead of one target versus all
non-target classes, they recursively split the logits into local pairs of classes and distil the
dense pairwise relations between them, with adaptive weights that depend on the rank difference within each pair and on the pair's total ranking score. LDRLD reports 77.20\% on ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4
\citep[Table~1]{xu2025ldrld}, 0.88 points above DKD as listed in the same table.
% src: LDRLD Table 1 (LDRLD 77.20; DKD 76.32)
DKD and NKD treat the non-target classes as one block; LDRLD argues that the order and the gaps
between the non-target classes carry information that this blocking discards. The cost is a number
of pairwise terms that grows with the number of classes considered, which the decay weights limit
but do not remove.

\paragraph{Generalised partitions.} Three preprints from 2025 revisit where DKD draws its line.
GDKD \citep{zheng2025gdkd} generalises the target/non-target split into a partition between the
teacher's top classes and the rest, and reports 77.76\% on our reference pair against 76.64\% for its own DKD run
\citep[Table~I]{zheng2025gdkd}. TopKD \citep{wang2025topkd} scales and decouples the teacher's top-$K$
logits and reports 78.18\% \citep[Table~2]{wang2025topkd}. Sort-KD \citep{limantoro2025sortkd} takes a
parameter-free route: it sorts the logits so that the teacher's mispredictions are corrected before
transfer. It reports no ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4 result; on ImageNet
ResNet34$\to$ResNet18, Sort-DKD reaches 71.84\% \citep[Table~II]{limantoro2025sortkd}.
% src: GDKD T I (77.76; own DKD 76.64); TopKD T2 (78.18); Sort-KD T II (Sort-DKD 71.84). All three CORE-P preprints.
DKD fixes the head of the split at the true class. GDKD and TopKD turn the size of that head into a
design choice, while Sort-KD edits the teacher's ordering itself, which puts it closer to RLD's
label-based masking than to DKD. GDKD and Sort-KD state acceptance at TNNLS and IEEE SPL, outside the
venues of our inclusion criteria, and GDKD's DKD baseline sits 0.32 points above DKD's own figure.

\paragraph{LTKD.} \citet{kim2026ltkd} apply decoupling to long-tailed training data, where the
teacher is biased towards head classes and gives weak supervision on tail classes. LTKD rewrites the
KD objective as a \emph{cross-group} term, which compares how teacher and student spread probability
over groups of classes (head, medium, tail), and a \emph{within-group} term, which compares the
distributions inside each group. It rebalances the cross-group term so that the teacher's head bias
is not copied, and reweights the within-group terms so that tail groups are not drowned out. On
CIFAR-100-LT with imbalance factor 100, ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4 reaches 51.08\% overall
and 27.21\% on tail classes, against 46.11\% and 13.25\% for DKD \citep[Table~1]{kim2026ltkd}.
% src: LTKD (arXiv 2506.18496v2) Table 1, gamma=100: LTKD All 51.08 / T 27.21; DKD 46.11 / 13.25
DKD separates the target class from the rest; LTKD separates groups of classes defined by their
training frequency, which makes it a decoupling method for class imbalance rather than a general
replacement for KD. The paper evaluates only long-tailed benchmarks, so it gives no number on the
balanced CIFAR-100 protocol of Table~\ref{tab:logit-sota}, and the group boundaries are an extra
design choice \citep{kim2026ltkd}.

\subsection{Rescaling the logits: temperature and standardisation}
\label{sec:logit-scale}

\paragraph{CTKD.} \citet{li2023ctkd} turn the fixed temperature of Eq.~\eqref{eq:softmax-T} into a
learnable quantity. A small module predicts $T$ and is trained \emph{adversarially}, through a
gradient-reversal layer, to maximise the distillation loss that the student minimises; a coefficient
that grows over training raises the difficulty over training, as in curriculum learning. CTKD is a
plug-in that adds about one parameter in its global form. Its gains are modest and consistent. On
ImageNet ResNet34$\to$ResNet18 it raises KD from 70.83\% to 71.32\% and DKD from 71.13\% to
71.51\% \citep[Table~4]{li2023ctkd}, and on CIFAR-100 the improvements over KD range from 0.33 to
1.12 points across eleven pairs \citep[Table~1]{li2023ctkd}.
% src: CTKD Table 4 (KD 70.83 -> 71.32; DKD 71.13 -> 71.51); CTKD Table 1 (+0.33 ... +1.12)
The paper does not report ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4; LSKD's reproduction gives KD+CTKD
73.39\% on that pair \citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd}. CTKD removes the grid search over $T$, but it
keeps one $T$ for teacher and student, so it does not address the mismatch in logit range that LSKD
targets.
% src: LSKD Table 2 (KD+CTKD 73.39)

\paragraph{WTTM.} \citet{zheng2024wttm} make the opposite move: they apply the temperature to the
teacher only and leave the student's softmax at $T=1$. They show that this \emph{transformed teacher
matching} (TTM) equals KD plus a R\'enyi-entropy regulariser on the student, which explains part of
KD's benefit as regularisation. WTTM then weights each sample by the power sum of the teacher's
transformed distribution, a measure of its smoothness. WTTM reports 76.06\% on our reference pair
\citep[Table~2]{zheng2024wttm} and 72.19\% on ImageNet ResNet34$\to$ResNet18
\citep[Table~5]{zheng2024wttm}.
% src: WTTM Table 2 (76.06); WTTM Table 5 (72.19)
WTTM is the cheapest change in this section, one line of code on top of KD, and its regulariser view
is useful when reasoning about why KD helps. On CIFAR-100 it is slightly below DKD and NKD, and its
larger gains come when combined with feature methods (77.36\% with ITRD,
\citealp[Table~2]{zheng2024wttm}).
% src: WTTM Table 2 (WTTM+ITRD 77.36)

\paragraph{LSKD.} \citet{sun2024lskd} argue that a shared temperature forces the student to match the
teacher's logit magnitudes exactly, although only the relations between logits matter for the
prediction, and a small student may not be able to reach the teacher's range. Their remedy is a
pre-processing step: each logit vector is z-score standardised (zero mean, unit standard deviation)
before the softmax, which amounts to a sample-specific temperature proportional to the logits'
standard deviation. Because it acts only on the inputs to the loss, it plugs into KD, CTKD, DKD and
MLKD. On ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4 it lifts KD from 73.33\% to 76.62\%, DKD from 76.32\%
to 77.01\% and MLKD from 77.08\% to 78.28\% \citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd}.
% src: LSKD Table 2 (KD+Ours 76.62; DKD+Ours 77.01; MLKD+Ours 78.28)
LSKD and CTKD both act on the temperature, but in different ways: CTKD learns one global value
on a curriculum, while LSKD sets it per sample from the logits and frees the student from matching
the teacher's scale. LSKD and DKD are complementary rather than competing, since standardisation
changes the inputs of the KL and decoupling changes its structure; the 0.69-point gain of
DKD+LS over DKD shows that they combine. Two caveats apply. The gains shrink on other pairs, for
example 0.13 points for DKD on VGG13$\to$VGG8 \citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd}, and other groups report
lower numbers for plain KD+LS on our pair (76.16\%, \citealp[Table~1]{xu2025ldrld}).
% src: LSKD Table 2 (DKD+Ours on VGG13->VGG8: +0.13); LDRLD Table 1 (LSKD 76.16)

\paragraph{LumiNet.} \citet{hossain2025luminet} also recalibrate the logits before distillation, but
with batch statistics instead of per-sample ones: each class logit is normalised by the mean and
variance of that class across the mini-batch, so a sample's ``perceived'' logits express how it
compares with the other samples in the batch. LumiNet reports 77.50\% on our reference pair, averaged
over five runs \citep[Table~1]{hossain2025luminet}, and an expected calibration error of 0.06 against 0.11 for KD on the same pair
\citep[Table~5]{hossain2025luminet}.
% src: LumiNet Table 1 (77.50, 5 trials); Table 5 (ACC/ECE: KD 73.33/0.11, Ours 77.50/0.06)
LSKD normalises within a sample and LumiNet across samples.
% INTERPRETATION
One likely consequence is
that LumiNet's targets depend on batch composition, so batch size and class balance within a batch
become hyper-parameters of the distillation signal.

\paragraph{Temperature schedules.} DTS \citep{islam2025dts} schedules the temperature from the gap
between the teacher's and the student's losses. CTKD learns $T$ adversarially; DTS instead ties it
to a measured quantity during training. Its reported gain is small: MLKD+DTS reaches
75.25\% against 75.03\% for MLKD in the same table \citep[Table~II]{islam2025dts}, and that MLKD row
is about two points below MLKD's own 77.08\%, so the result is hard to place against
Table~\ref{tab:logit-sota}.
% src: DTS T II (MLKD+DTS 75.25; MLKD 75.03). CORE-P preprint.

\subsection{Replacing or enriching the KL divergence}
\label{sec:logit-div}

\paragraph{DIST.} \citet{huang2022dist} start from the capacity-gap problem: when the teacher is much
stronger, exact matching of its probabilities with KL becomes too hard for the student. DIST
replaces KL with a correlation loss. It maximises the Pearson correlation between the student's and
the teacher's predictions for each sample (inter-class relation) and for each class across the batch
(intra-class relation), which keeps the ranking and relative spread of predictions while ignoring
shift and scale. DIST reports 76.31\% on our reference pair \citep[Table~5]{huang2022dist} and
72.07\% on ImageNet ResNet34$\to$ResNet18 \citep[Table~2]{huang2022dist}, and its largest gains are
with strong teachers and strong training recipes.
% src: DIST Table 5 (76.31); DIST Table 2 (72.07)
% INTERPRETATION
DIST and LSKD appear to reach shift- and scale-invariance by different routes, DIST by its loss and LSKD by
pre-processing before an unchanged KL. The authors note that DIST is less
effective for binary classification, where there is little inter-class relation to transfer
\citep{huang2022dist}.

\paragraph{MLKD.} \citet{jin2023mlkd} align predictions at three levels: per instance, across the
batch (sample-to-sample similarity), and per class (class-to-class correlation). They also
\emph{augment} each prediction by computing it at several temperatures, so that one output yields
several target distributions. MLKD reports 77.08\% on ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4
\citep[Table~2]{jin2023mlkd}, which rises to 78.28\% with LSKD's standardisation
\citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd}, the highest logit-only number among the CORE methods in
Table~\ref{tab:logit-sota}.
% src: MLKD Table 2 (77.08); LSKD Table 2 (MLKD+Ours 78.28)
MLKD's batch- and class-level terms are close in spirit to DIST's intra-class relation. Part of its
gain over DKD may come from the multi-temperature prediction augmentation rather than from the
multi-level alignment itself; we did not find a comparison against DKD with the same set of
temperatures.
% HYPOTHESIS: the multi-temperature augmentation explains part of MLKD's gain.

\paragraph{WKD.} \citet{lv2024wkd} point out that KL compares the probability of each class only with
the probability of the same class, so it cannot use the fact that, say, ``dog'' is close to other
mammals. WKD-L replaces KL with a discrete Wasserstein distance whose ground cost comes from
category interrelations measured in feature space; WKD-F is a feature-level counterpart based on
Gaussian Wasserstein distances. WKD-L reports 76.53\% on our reference pair and 77.28\% when combined
with WKD-F \citep[Table~11]{lv2024wkd}, and 72.49\% on ImageNet ResNet34$\to$ResNet18
\citep[Table~4]{lv2024wkd}.
% src: WKD Table 11 (WKD-L 76.53; WKD-L+WKD-F 77.28); WKD Table 4 (ImageNet WKD-L 72.49)
WKD shares DIST's goal of using cross-class structure but encodes it in the cost of moving
probability mass rather than in a correlation. The Wasserstein computation and the estimation of the
cost matrix make it more expensive than the KL-based methods; \citet{lv2024wkd} report its training
latency on ImageNet.

\paragraph{EKD.} \citet{xiang2025ekd} model each network's output as a Dirichlet distribution over the
class probabilities, a second-order distribution in the sense of evidential deep learning. EKD distils
both the expectation of the teacher's Dirichlet (a first-order term close to KD) and the Dirichlet
itself, so the student also inherits how certain the teacher is. It reports 77.21\% on our reference
pair \citep[Table~1]{xiang2025ekd} and 72.54\% on ImageNet ResNet50$\to$MobileNet
\citep[Table~3]{xiang2025ekd}.
% INTERPRETATION from the ``EDL'' teacher rows in EKD Table 1.
Judging from the evidential teacher rows of their Table~1, EKD appears to need a teacher trained or adapted with the evidential loss, which
is extra work compared with reusing a standard pretrained teacher.
% src: EKD Table 1 (77.21; EDL teacher 79.53 vs softmax 79.42); EKD Table 3 (72.54)

\paragraph{Reformulated KL losses.} Several papers keep a KL-type objective but change its parts.
DKL \citep{cui2024dkl} shows that KL equals a weighted mean-squared error between logits plus a
cross-entropy with soft labels, and uses the decomposition to symmetrise the loss; it reports 76.59\%
on our reference pair \citep[Table~13]{cui2024dkl}. Its journal extension, GKL \citep{cui2026gkl},
reports 76.83\% \citep[Table~17]{cui2026gkl}, and both reach 71.91\% on ImageNet
ResNet34$\to$ResNet18. BDD \citep{qi2025bdd} adds a reverse-KL term for the classes to which the
teacher gives small probability, which forward KL largely ignores; plain BDD reports 76.21\% and
MLD+BDD 77.22\% \citep[Table~1]{qi2025bdd}. ABKD \citep{wang2025abkd} replaces KL with an
$\alpha$-$\beta$-divergence whose two parameters control how the student allocates probability
mass; it reports our pair only in a figure, so we quote no
number. RCKD \citep{wang2026rckd} targets the capacity mismatch that also motivates DIST and replaces
KL with a cosine-type loss, reporting 76.89\% \citep[Table~1]{wang2026rckd}. GenDD
\citep{cui2025gendd} goes furthest from KL: it treats distillation as matching a conditional
generative distribution and reports 77.47\% on our pair and 72.38\% on ImageNet
\citep[Tables~5 and~2]{cui2025gendd}.
% src: DKL T13 (76.59), T4 (IN 71.91); GKL T17 (76.83), T5 (IN 71.91); BDD T1 (76.21; MLD+BDD 77.22);
% ABKD Fig. 5 only; RCKD T1 (76.89), T3 (IN 72.02); GenDD T5 (77.47), T2 (IN 72.38). BDD, GenDD: CORE-P.
DIST and WKD change what is matched; DKL/GKL, BDD and ABKD keep per-class matching and change
how the mismatch on each class is penalised. DKL/GKL and BDD stay closest to KD and are the easiest to
swap into an existing pipeline; ABKD adds two divergence parameters to tune, and GenDD changes the
training target more deeply.
% INTERPRETATION: "easiest to swap" and "more deeply" are our reading of the mechanisms above.

\paragraph{PLD.} \citet{bassam2025pld} recast logit distillation as list-wise ranking. Their loss is a
Plackett--Luce likelihood over a class ordering that puts the true label first and orders the other
classes by the teacher; the authors state that it is convex and translation-invariant and that it
subsumes weighted cross-entropy, so it needs no separate distillation weight $\lambda$. We read only
the NeurIPS abstract page, so these properties are the authors' claims and we do
not quote a number.
% VERIFIED: as the authors' claims on the abstract page only. PLD is relevant to \S\ref{sec:kd_moo}: by folding the label and the teacher into
one target it removes the CE--KD trade-off of Eq.~\eqref{eq:kd-objective} instead of balancing it.

\paragraph{Ranking and class-correlation losses.} PLD is not the only ranking objective. The Kendall-$\tau$
loss of \citet{guan2025rankingkd} is a plug-in term that aligns the order of the teacher's and
the student's logits; stacked on MLKD it reports 77.25\% on our pair \citep[Table~2]{guan2025rankingkd}
and on NKD 72.06\% on ImageNet \citep[Table~13]{guan2025rankingkd}. Where PLD replaces the loss with a
list-wise likelihood, the Kendall-$\tau$ term keeps KL and adds a pairwise order constraint next to it.
AR-KD \citep{jiang2026arkd} learns class correlations through adaptive reciprocal learning between
teacher and student, reporting 77.29\% on our pair and 72.13\% on ImageNet
\citep[Tables~1 and~2]{jiang2026arkd}; its target is the class-to-class structure that WKD encodes in a
transport cost and MLKD in a class-level correlation.
% src: Ranking loss T2 (MLKD+ranking 77.25), T13 (IN NKD+ranking 72.06); AR-KD T1 (77.29), T2 (IN 72.13).
% AR-KD: CORE-P preprint (author-stated CIKM 2026).

\paragraph{Batch-level consistency.} Two methods add batch structure to instance-level KL, as MLKD's
batch term does. SKD \citep{jeong2026skd} pairs an instance KL with alignment of the batch Gram matrix
and reports 78.33\% on our pair \citep[Table~1]{jeong2026skd}, the highest single-method logit number
we found. Its own KD baseline, however, is 74.75\%, about 1.4 points above the 73.33\% of
Table~\ref{tab:logit-sota}, so part of the margin may be protocol; on ImageNet it reports 71.13\%
\citep[Table~3]{jeong2026skd}. IKD \citep{wang2025ikd} is a debiased batch-level consistency
regulariser used as a plug-in; NKD+IKD reports 77.10\% on our pair and 72.20\% on ImageNet
\citep[Tables~2 and~3]{wang2025ikd}.
% src: SKD T1 (78.33; KD row 74.75, DKD row 76.51), T3 (IN 71.13); IKD T2 (NKD+IKD 77.10), T3 (IN 72.20).
% HYPOTHESIS: SKD's margin over MLKD is partly due to its stronger baseline protocol; untested.

\paragraph{Per-sample weighting.} Other methods keep the loss and change how much each sample
contributes. IPWD \citep{niu2022ipwd} weights the distillation loss by inverse propensity, so that
samples under-represented in the transfer set count more; it reports 76.03\% on our pair and 71.88\%
on ImageNet \citep[Tables~2 and~4]{niu2022ipwd}. TGeo-KD \citep{hu2024tgeokd} sets the weight of each
sample from a trilateral geometry between the student, the teacher and the label (77.27\% on our
pair, \citealp[Table~1]{hu2024tgeokd}; 72.89\% on ImageNet, \citealp[Table~2]{hu2024tgeokd}). EA-KD
\citep{su2025eakd} weights samples by entropy; alone it reports 75.46\%, and stacked as EA-MLD+LS it
reaches 78.38\% \citep[Table~2]{su2025eakd}. ADG-KD \citep{li2025adgkd} fuses teacher guidance with
instance guidance through learned factors and reports 77.44\% alone and 77.51\% on MLKD
\citep[Table~1]{li2025adgkd}. These differ from WTTM's power-sum weights in what defines a hard sample:
a propensity, a geometric relation to the label, an entropy or a learned factor. TGeo-KD and EA-KD are
analysed with the reliability methods in \S\ref{sec:reliability}. e$^2$KD \citep{parchami2024e2kd} adds
a different signal, the similarity of the two networks' explanation maps, to logit KD; at full ImageNet
data it matches KD at 71.8\%, and its reported gains come in the few-shot setting
\citep[Table~1]{parchami2024e2kd}.
% src: IPWD T2 (76.03), T4 (71.88); TGeo-KD T1 (77.27, per registry), T2 (72.89); EA-KD T2 (EA-KD 75.46;
% EA-MLD+LS 78.38); ADG-KD T1 (77.44; MLKD+ADG 77.51), T2 (IN 72.22); e2KD T1 (71.8 = KD at full data).

\paragraph{RLD.} \citet{sun2025rld} address a problem that all methods above inherit: when the teacher
is wrong, its distribution contradicts the label, and the student receives conflicting targets. RLD
uses the label to refine what is transferred. It distils a binary \emph{sample confidence} and a
\emph{masked correlation} in which every class that the teacher ranks at or above the true class is masked out,
so useful class relations survive while the wrong ranking does not. It reports 76.64\% on our
reference pair \citep[Table~1]{sun2025rld}, 0.32 points above DKD in the same table.
% src: RLD Table 1 (RLD 76.64; DKD 76.32)
RLD changes which part of the teacher signal to trust rather than the divergence, and we compare it
with other reliability methods in \S\ref{sec:reliability}.

\subsection{Comparison and outlook}
\label{sec:logit-compare}

% INTERPRETATION below unless cited.
Table~\ref{tab:logit-sota} summarises the methods.
Across these papers, LSKD's standardisation improves KD, DKD and MLKD \citep[Table~2]{sun2024lskd},
and SDD wraps KD, DKD and NKD \citep{wei2024sdd}, so decomposition, rescaling and the choice of
divergence behave as separable design axes. The 2024--2025 methods report 76.0--77.5\% on
ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4, a range comparable to the gap between different papers'
reproductions of one method (\S\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}); the table therefore does not establish
a single best method, while the step of about three points from KD (73.33\%) to the 2022 methods is
consistent across sources. Recent work has shifted from the divergence itself to which teacher signal
to trust (RLD here; \S\ref{sec:reliability}), to class imbalance in the teacher (LTKD), and to how the
distillation term is balanced against the task loss (PLD; \S\ref{sec:kd_moo}). DKD and KD+LSKD are
low-overhead baselines with public code, and both are re-reported by other
papers (EKD and LDRLD list both, \citealp[Table~1]{xiang2025ekd,xu2025ldrld}); MLKD+LSKD reports the highest value in the table but adds loss terms and temperatures.
Among the methods added by the systematic search, SKD (78.33\%, \citealp[Table~1]{jeong2026skd}) and
EA-MLD+LS (78.38\%, \citealp[Table~2]{su2025eakd}) report slightly higher values, the first with a stronger KD
baseline and the second stacked on two other methods; most plug-ins of 2024--2026 (ranking, IKD, ADG-KD,
BDD, EA-KD) report their best numbers on top of MLKD or NKD rather than alone. The table keeps
representative rows; \S\ref{sec:benchmarks} lists the full included set.
