\section{Open Challenges and Future Directions}
\label{sec:future}

The previous sections leave several questions that the published results cannot answer. Each
paragraph below names one, points to the evidence for it in this survey, and ends with a direction
that could be tested on the standard protocol. The directions are recommendations, not findings.

\paragraph{A comparable leaderboard.}
Section~\ref{sec:bench-protocol} lists three reasons why the CIFAR-100 and ImageNet tables are not a
controlled comparison: papers report means, best runs or single runs; many top rows are stacked on
another method; and some rows change the teacher, the data or the KD baseline. The size of the
problem is clear from the baselines alone. On ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4, classical KD is
73.33\% in DKD's table \citep{zhao2022dkd}, 74.75\% in SKD's \citep{jeong2026skd} and 76.25\% in
DiSK's \citep{kag2023disk}, a spread wider than the gaps between most single logit methods in
Tables~\ref{tab:bench-cifar-a} and~\ref{tab:bench-cifar-b}. Section~\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}
already notes that gaps of 0.3--0.5 points sit inside the seed spread. Recipe choices also move results: \citet{beyer2022patientkd} identify training length and consistent teacher and student views as consequential, so a comparison of direction rules must hold the recipe fixed. The coverage is also thin.
The WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2 column is nearly empty outside data-free work, and only three included
papers were read for ResNet50$\to$MobileNetV1 on ImageNet \citep{mishra2026mcd,huang2025distplus,lin2026lb}.
\emph{Direction:} re-run the methods with public code from Section~\ref{sec:benchmarks} in one code
base, with fixed teachers, at least five seeds and both the ResNet and WRN pairs, and report whether
the published order of the top ten single methods survives (for example, by rank correlation with
the tables).

\paragraph{What stacking measures.}
The five highest ranked CIFAR-100 rows include three stacked configurations: DeepKD on CRLD,
EA-MLD with label smoothing, and MLKD with logit standardisation \citep{huang2025deepkd,su2025eakd,sun2024lskd}.
Several plug-ins report a large stand-alone gap: EA-KD alone gives 75.46\% against 78.38\% stacked,
and NCKD gives 77.23\% alone against 78.18\% on top of SimKD \citep{zhang2025nckd}. A stacked row
shows that two ideas can be combined. It does not show that the new idea is better than an existing
one, and it does not say whether the two gains add up or overlap. We found no included paper that reports a full
grid of plug-ins against bases. \emph{Direction:} take a small set of bases (KD, DKD, MLKD) and
plug-ins (logit standardisation, DOT, a reliability weight), train every pairing, and report whether
the stacked gain equals the sum of the two single gains or falls short of it.

\paragraph{Which teacher signal to trust.}
Section~\ref{sec:reliability} shows that the reliability methods disagree about what reliability
means. CTSL checks label agreement, CCKD uses the probability on the label, EA-KD uses entropy,
TGeo-KD uses three-way geometry, and CHAD uses alignment with a validation gradient
\citep{meng2019conditionalts,mishra2021cckd,su2025eakd,hu2024tgeokd,sumit2026chad}. Several of them
were evaluated on speech, generation or summarisation rather than on the image classification
protocol, and the classification ones that do report on ResNet8$\times$4 land between 75.5\% and
76.6\% (Section~\ref{sec:bench-families}). A teacher-side group (MCMI, DJIP, SoTeacher) changes the
teacher instead, which makes it hard to place on the same table \citep{ye2024mcmi,chen2026djip,dong2024soteacher}.
Two older results argue against equating teacher accuracy with usefulness: \citet{yuan2020labelkd} report gains from poorly trained and reversed teachers, and \citet{stanton2021kdwork} show that teacher--student fidelity and student generalisation need not improve together, so a lower KD loss is not itself evidence of a better student. We found no paper that tests whether these signals pick the same samples. \emph{Direction:} on one fixed CIFAR-100
teacher, compute each signal per training sample, measure how much the selected or down-weighted
sets overlap, and check which signal best predicts the samples whose removal from the distillation
term improves the student.

\paragraph{How to combine the task and distillation objectives.}
Section~\ref{sec:kd_moo_critical} found that the optimiser-side methods split into two camps.
SCKD, SCOPE, TPKD, MEMA and PCD let the label gradient win; DOT and DeepKD let distillation lead;
DTO-KD and AE-KD balance the two. All report gains, and no paper compares a label-first rule with a
teacher-first rule under one protocol. Only DOT, among the distillation papers we read, compares with
a sweep of fixed task weights \citep{zhao2023dot}, while three multi-task studies found that tuned
scalarisation is hard to beat \citep{xin2022mtohelp,kurin2022unitary,royer2023scalarization}. PCD
\citep{varam2026pcd} has no reported distillation result, so it is an open
case of the same question, not an answer to it. \emph{Direction:} implement one label-first rule,
one teacher-first rule and one symmetric rule in the same code base, run each on top of KD and of a
strong logit loss, and compare them with a weighted sum whose weight is tuned on held-out data.

\paragraph{Is there conflict to resolve?}
The gradient-combination methods assume that the task and distillation gradients often point in
different directions. The only direct measurement we found is DHO's, with a zero-shot CLIP teacher
and 16 labelled examples per class, where the final-epoch cosine at the classifier is $-0.76$
\citep{kang2025dho}. For the full-label CIFAR-100 or ImageNet protocol with a trained CNN teacher
we found none. The PCD authors attribute their largest pruning gains to objective scale rather
than conflict \citep{varam2026pcd}, and TPKD loses only 0.22 points on CIFAR-100 without its
projection \citep{yuan2026tpkd}. So a reported gain may come from rescaling the losses.
\emph{Direction:} log the per-step cosine and norm ratio between the CE and KD gradients for the
standard CIFAR-100 pairs, then compare each conflict-handling rule with a version that only applies
its implied rescaling; if the two match, the gain is due to scale.

\paragraph{Data-free distillation and the ImageNet gap.}
The data-free methods in Table~\ref{tab:bench-datafree} report on WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2 only, and
their results run from 61.44\% to 74.52\% \citep{liu2024ssdkd,wang2024kdci}, with differing epoch
budgets and one row that mixes collected and generated data \citep{tang2025hidfd}. They share no
pair with the main CIFAR-100 ranking, so the cost of losing the training data is not measured
against the data-based methods. ImageNet evidence is thin in a different way: most ResNet34$\to$ResNet18
rows fall between 71.0\% and 73.0\%, close to DKD's 71.70\% \citep{zhao2023dot}, so CIFAR-100 gains of
two or more points mostly shrink to under one. \emph{Direction:} report data-free and data-based
methods on the same ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4 and WRN pairs with the same teacher, and
report, for each method in the CIFAR-100 top ten, the ratio of its ImageNet gain over KD to its
CIFAR-100 gain.

\paragraph{Metrics beyond top-1 accuracy.}
Almost every table in this survey ranks by top-1 accuracy. Section~\ref{sec:prelim-temperature}
separates the KD temperature from calibration \citep{guo2017calibration}, yet the only expected calibration error quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:logit} is LumiNet's, 0.06 against 0.11 for KD \citep{hossain2025luminet}.
The reliability methods aim to stop the student copying the teacher's errors, but they are judged
by accuracy, not by whether those errors are transferred. Robustness is outside the scope of this
survey, though Section~\ref{sec:emerging} notes that a more robust teacher need not give a more
robust student \citep{lee2026robustteachers}. Training cost is also rarely reported, although DOT
needs two backward passes per step and searched distillers need a search per pair
\citep{zhao2023dot,li2023kdzero}. \emph{Direction:} for the re-run proposed above, report expected
calibration error, accuracy on a corrupted test set, agreement with the teacher on its wrong
predictions, and wall-clock time per epoch alongside top-1, and check whether the ranking by
accuracy holds on each of them.
