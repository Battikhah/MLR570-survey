\section{Open Challenges and Future Directions}
\label{sec:future}

Each paragraph names an open question, its evidence, and a testable direction (a recommendation, not a finding).

\paragraph{A comparable leaderboard.}
The tables are not a controlled comparison (Section~\ref{sec:bench-protocol}), and the baselines show how far apart papers sit. On ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4,
classical KD is 73.33\% in DKD's table \citep{zhao2022dkd}, 74.75\% in SKD's \citep{jeong2026skd} and 76.25\%
in DiSK's \citep{kag2023disk}, a spread wider than the gaps between most single logit methods in
Table~\ref{tab:app-methods}, while gaps of 0.3--0.5 points sit inside the seed spread
(Section~\ref{sec:prelim-benchmarks}). Recipe choices also move results: \citet{beyer2022patientkd} identify
training length and consistent teacher and student views as consequential, so a comparison of direction rules
must hold the recipe fixed. Stacking adds a second problem. Three of the five highest CIFAR-100 rows are stacked
(DeepKD on CRLD, EA-MLD with label smoothing, MLKD with logit standardisation;
\citealp{huang2025deepkd,su2025eakd,sun2024lskd}), and several plug-ins lose much of their gain alone: EA-KD gives
75.46\% against 78.38\% stacked, and NCKD 77.23\% against 78.18\% on SimKD \citep{zhang2025nckd}. A stacked row
shows that two ideas combine, not that the new one beats an existing one or that the gains add up; we found no
included paper with a full grid of plug-ins against bases. Coverage is thin: only three included papers were read for
ResNet50$\to$MobileNetV1 \citep{mishra2026mcd,huang2025distplus,lin2026lb}. \emph{Direction:} re-run the methods
with public code in one code base, with fixed teachers and at least five seeds; check by rank correlation whether
the published order of the top ten single methods survives, and train every pairing of a few bases (KD, DKD, MLKD) and plug-ins (logit standardisation, DOT, a reliability
weight) to test whether stacked gains add up.

\paragraph{Which teacher signal to trust.}
The reliability methods of Section~\ref{sec:reliability} disagree about what reliability means. CTSL checks
label agreement, CCKD uses the probability on the label, EA-KD uses entropy, TGeo-KD uses three-way geometry,
and CHAD uses alignment with a validation gradient
\citep{meng2019conditionalts,mishra2021cckd,su2025eakd,hu2024tgeokd,sumit2026chad}. Those that report on ResNet8$\times$4
land between 75.5\% and 76.6\% (Section~\ref{sec:bench-families}). A teacher-side group (MCMI, DJIP, SoTeacher) changes the teacher
instead \citep{ye2024mcmi,chen2026djip,dong2024soteacher}. Two older results argue against equating teacher
accuracy with usefulness: \citet{yuan2020labelkd} report gains from poorly trained and reversed teachers, and
\citet{stanton2021kdwork} show that teacher--student fidelity and student generalisation need not improve
together, so a lower KD loss is not itself evidence of a better student. We found no paper that tests whether
these signals pick the same samples. \emph{Direction:} on one fixed teacher, compute each signal per sample,
measure the overlap of the selected sets, and check which best predicts the samples whose removal from the
distillation term helps the student.

\paragraph{How to combine the objectives, and whether they conflict.}
Section~\ref{sec:kd_moo_critical} found that the optimiser-side methods split into camps: SCKD, SCOPE, TPKD,
MEMA and PCD let the label gradient win; DOT and DeepKD let distillation lead; DTO-KD and AE-KD balance the two.
No paper compares them under one protocol, and only DOT compares with a sweep of fixed task weights \citep{zhao2023dot}, while
three multi-task studies found tuned scalarisation hard to beat
\citep{xin2022mtohelp,kurin2022unitary,royer2023scalarization}. PCD \citep{varam2026pcd} has no reported
distillation result, so it is an open case of the question, not an answer. These methods also assume that the
two gradients often conflict. The only direct measurement we found is DHO's, with a zero-shot CLIP teacher and 16
labelled examples per class, where the final-epoch cosine at the classifier is $-0.76$ \citep{kang2025dho}; for
the full-label protocol with a trained CNN teacher we found none. The PCD authors attribute their largest pruning
gains to objective scale rather than conflict \citep{varam2026pcd}, and TPKD loses only 0.22 points on
CIFAR-100 without its projection \citep{yuan2026tpkd}, so a reported gain may come from rescaling the losses.
\emph{Direction:} run one rule of each camp on top of KD and a strong logit loss against a tuned weighted sum,
log the per-step cosine and norm ratio of the CE and KD gradients, and compare each rule with one that applies
only its implied rescaling.

\paragraph{Data-free distillation and the ImageNet gap.}
The data-free methods report on WRN-40-2$\to$WRN-16-2 only, from 61.44\% to 74.52\%
\citep{liu2024ssdkd,wang2024kdci}, with differing epoch budgets and one row that mixes collected and generated
data \citep{tang2025hidfd}. They share no pair with the main CIFAR-100 ranking, so the cost of losing the
training data is not measured. On ImageNet, most ResNet34$\to$ResNet18 rows fall between 71.0\% and 73.0\%,
close to DKD's 71.70\% \citep{zhao2023dot}, so CIFAR-100 gains of two or more points mostly shrink to under one.
\emph{Direction:} report data-free and data-based methods on the same pairs with the same teacher, and report for
each CIFAR-100 top-ten method the ratio of its ImageNet gain over KD to its CIFAR-100 gain.

\paragraph{Metrics and settings beyond top-1 accuracy.}
Almost every table here ranks by top-1 accuracy. Section~\ref{sec:prelim-temperature} separates the KD
temperature from calibration \citep{guo2017calibration}, yet the only expected calibration error quoted in
Section~\ref{sec:logit} is LumiNet's, 0.06 against 0.11 for KD \citep{hossain2025luminet}. The reliability
methods aim to stop the student copying the teacher's errors, but are judged by accuracy, not by whether those
errors transfer. Training cost is rarely reported, though DOT needs two backward passes per step and searched
distillers a search per pair \citep{zhao2023dot,li2023kdzero}.
\label{sec:emerging}Neighbouring settings are outside this survey's scope but raise the same questions: in LLM
distillation the student's own samples become training data, and UP-MOPD applies PCGrad-style projection to
per-teacher updates \citep{gu2024minillm,zhu2026upmopd}; with vision--language teachers, CLIP-KD finds feature
mimicry a strong baseline and DHO removes CE--KD conflict with separate heads \citep{yang2024clipkd,kang2025dho};
a more robust teacher need not give a more robust student \citep{lee2026robustteachers}, and CA-AT (an
unverified preprint) projects adversarial gradients in the priority style \citep{xue2024caat}; federated
distillation transfers knowledge without a shared training set \citep{luo2023dfrd,jang2025fedgo}.
\emph{Direction:} in the re-run above, also report calibration error, corrupted-test accuracy, agreement with
the teacher's wrong predictions and time per epoch, and check whether the ranking holds on each.
