\section{Teacher Reliability and Selective Transfer}
\label{sec:reliability}

Teacher reliability is not a single property. Agreement with a label, probability assigned to that label, calibration, and usefulness to a particular student measure different things. Conditional Teacher-Student Learning (CTSL) selects teacher targets only when the teacher predicts the observed label, while Confidence Conditioned Knowledge Distillation (CCKD) uses the teacher's probability on that label as a sample weight or to blend teacher and one-hot targets; its self-regulation variant can omit examples the student already handles easily \citep{meng2019conditionalts,mishra2021cckd}. CTSL therefore makes a discrete correctness decision, whereas CCKD retains graded confidence. CCKD is available as an arXiv preprint; no later venue is established here. Both methods depend on observed labels, so label errors can make their reliability signal wrong. Correctness alone also says nothing about calibration, student compatibility, or the value of the teacher's other class probabilities. % INTERPRETATION: limits of a top-class correctness gate.

Hard Gate KD extends selective routing to language generation by comparing the student's probability on the true token or sentence with the corresponding calibrated-teacher quantity, then switching between teacher and label supervision \citep{lee2022hardgatekd}. Unlike CTSL's teacher-correctness test, this gate incorporates the student's current state and calibration; unlike CCKD's soft weights, it makes a hard decision. The label branch may be inactive when teacher supervision is selected, so the gate does not guarantee an independent ground-truth loss on every example. Its evidence is specific to the reported language-model setting. Calibration itself does not guarantee reliability after distribution shift, and confidence estimates can change under shift. The post-hoc temperature used to calibrate confidence also has a different role from the KD temperature used to soften logits; treating those as the same parameter would conflate a probability correction with a transfer-target transformation \citep{guo2017calibration,ovadia2019shiftuncertainty}.

For multiple teachers, CA-MKD uses each teacher's cross-entropy against the label to assign sample-wise relative weights and transfers logits and intermediate features \citep{zhang2022camkd}. This replaces CTSL's single-teacher switch with a weighted ensemble, which can retain partial information from several teachers; however, the paper does not establish that an independent student cross-entropy term is retained on every example. % VERIFIED: inspected paper does not settle this loss detail.

Recent methods broaden the reliability signal beyond a teacher-only threshold. TGeo-KD estimates a per-sample teacher/label fusion ratio from the geometry among student, teacher, and label predictions. Relative to CTSL, it can retain a soft mixture instead of replacing teacher guidance wholesale when the teacher is judged unreliable; unlike CCKD, the mixing weight comes from three-way geometry rather than only teacher probability on the label. This makes it useful when student state is relevant to deciding how much to imitate, but the ratio remains a proxy and adds a geometry-based decision rule to tune \citep{hu2024tgeokd}.

EA-KD uses teacher and student entropy to weight examples, making its estimate of reliability responsive to both predictive distributions rather than only teacher-label agreement. It is a per-sample weighting alternative to TGeo-KD's explicit teacher/student/label geometry and to Hard Gate KD's binary routing; the trade-off is that entropy does not identify which class relations are trustworthy or prove that an update helps validation performance. It up-weights high-entropy samples, treating them as potentially more informative without a discrete sample-selection threshold \citep{su2025eakd}.

LTKD addresses reliability at the group level: it rebalances cross-group information and reweights within-group KL so a head-biased teacher does not suppress tail classes. That differs from EA-KD and CCKD, which adapt sample influence without explicitly correcting group imbalance, and from RLD, which repairs individual teacher logits using labels. LTKD is relevant when teacher failure is systematic across groups; its evidence should not be read as a general solution to calibration or student-specific usefulness \citep{kim2026ltkd}.

AGRE-KD selects teachers through gradients evaluated against a biased model and targets worst-group performance. Compared with CA-MKD's label-based sample-wise relative weights, AGRE-KD uses gradient information and an explicit group-robust objective; compared with LTKD, it selects among teachers rather than rebalancing the knowledge from one biased teacher. This makes it appropriate when several teachers are available and group performance is the concern, while the method's selection rule and evidence are specific to biased-group evaluation \citep{kenfack2025agrekd}.

TPKD takes a task-preservation route: it projects teacher directions to preserve task margins rather than estimating reliability from confidence or entropy. Unlike TGeo-KD and EA-KD, it addresses whether a teacher update threatens task performance, not whether the teacher's output appears uncertain; it therefore connects more directly to the asymmetric update rules in §\ref{sec:kd_moo}. This approach can be useful when preserving the student's task objective has priority over full imitation, but it requires a margin-based projection and cannot be compared as a confidence estimator. TPKD is currently available as a 2026 preprint, and its abstract does not specify the teacher/student setting, so its result should not be generalized beyond that evidence \citep{yuan2026tpkd}.

Reliability can also be addressed by repairing targets or by estimating student-specific usefulness. Label Revision and Data Selection (LR-DS) combines label-guided teacher-label revision with influence-based data selection. Examples outside the selected distillation subset remain under label supervision, so it differs from CTSL and Hard Gate KD, which route teacher versus label supervision per example, and from RLD, which refines logits rather than selecting a subset. LR-DS is useful when both noisy teacher targets and harmful examples are concerns; selection can reduce exposure to questionable targets, but its outcome depends on the revision and influence procedures. The final journal loss was not checked line by line against the inspected preprint, so no stronger claim about the exact retained CE term is made \citep{lan2025labelrevision}.

RLD uses labels to refine teacher logits, preserving selected class relations instead of discarding every output when the teacher's top prediction is wrong. It differs from CTSL's hard switch because the student can still receive information from a teacher whose top class is incorrect, and from CCKD's scalar confidence weighting because RLD changes the distribution being transferred. This repair is useful when the ranking is wrong but some non-top-class information may remain useful; however, refining selected correlations does not show that every retained relation benefits the student. Its evidence covers CIFAR-100 and ImageNet classification, not other domains \citep{sun2025rld}.

CaRE-KD uses CaRE-Divergence to switch between forward and reverse KL according to teacher–student confidence, then applies Revival as a batch-level rejection rule that suppresses updates when the teacher is more epistemically uncertain than the student. Unlike EA-KD's high-entropy sample weighting, Revival can reject an entire batch update; this makes the method relevant when teacher uncertainty is the concern, though its confidence estimates and rejection threshold add design choices \citep{sengupta2026casd}.

CHAD estimates sample usefulness from alignment between a distillation gradient and a validation-loss gradient, then learns sample-specific weights. It differs from teacher-only confidence methods because its signal depends on the student and validation objective, and from LR-DS because it reweights samples rather than revising labels or selecting a fixed subset. In the paper's BanSum comparison, teacher-confidence and surface-agreement gates each remained within 0.0001 ROUGE-L of cross-entropy, while CHAD improved over standard KD by 0.0173 ROUGE-L. The paper also reports a separate multilingual result for EWAD+CPDP, not CHAD \citep{sumit2026chad}. % VERIFIED: CHAD is a 2026 arXiv preprint; BanSum table reports the cited deltas.

\paragraph{Comparing reliability signals.} CCKD and EA-KD give graded weights, whereas CTSL and Hard Gate KD make routing decisions. SCKD gates teacher guidance by the similarity between KD and student-objective gradients; RLD repairs label-conditioned logits, LR-DS selects examples, and CHAD learns weights from validation-gradient alignment. These choices imply different failure cases: a label-based gate can inherit annotation errors, a confidence-based weight can trust a confident error, and a gradient-based proxy can vary with the validation objective. Group-level methods add another unit of analysis: aggregate bias can be hidden by a reasonable average accuracy, while a group correction can be unnecessary when performance is balanced. That distinction helps explain why LTKD and AGRE-KD should be assessed with group metrics, rather than ranked beside sample-level gates using only an overall score \citep{kim2026ltkd,kenfack2025agrekd}. The methods answer related but non-equivalent questions, and their evaluation settings should remain attached to any comparison \citep{meng2019conditionalts,mishra2021cckd,lee2022hardgatekd,su2025eakd,sun2025rld,lan2025labelrevision,sumit2026chad,zhu2021sckd}.

% INTERPRETATION: selection is most attractive when harmful teacher targets can be replaced with reliable labels; target repair is more appropriate when useful class relations may survive a wrong top prediction; group-aware approaches fit systematic group bias; task-preserving projection addresses interference with the student's objective.
These are method-selection heuristics, not a ranking: the papers use different tasks, including speech adaptation, generation, classification, group-robust benchmarks, and summarisation, so they do not establish a common protocol \citep{meng2019conditionalts,lee2022hardgatekd,sun2025rld,kim2026ltkd,kenfack2025agrekd,yuan2026tpkd,sumit2026chad}.
