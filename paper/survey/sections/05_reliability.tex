\section{Teacher Reliability and Selective Transfer}
\label{sec:reliability}

The methods assess teacher reliability using different signals (Table~\ref{tab:reliability}). Several of the papers come from speech, text generation or summarisation; they are background for the mechanism, outside our image-classification scope, and their numbers are not compared with the tables of \S\ref{sec:benchmarks}.

Conditional Teacher-Student Learning (CTSL) uses teacher targets only when the teacher predicts the observed label, while Confidence Conditioned KD (CCKD, an arXiv preprint) uses the teacher's probability on that label as a sample weight or to blend teacher and one-hot targets, and its self-regulation variant can skip examples the student already handles \citep{meng2019conditionalts,mishra2021cckd}. CTSL makes a discrete correctness decision; CCKD keeps graded confidence. Both rely on observed labels, so label errors corrupt the signal, and correctness says nothing about calibration, student compatibility, or the value of the other class probabilities. % INTERPRETATION: limits of a top-class correctness gate.

Hard Gate KD routes language-generation supervision by comparing the student's probability on the true token or sentence with the calibrated teacher's, switching between teacher and label \citep{lee2022hardgatekd}. Unlike CTSL it uses the student's state and calibration, and the label branch may be inactive when the teacher is selected, so no per-example ground-truth loss is guaranteed. The post-hoc calibration temperature plays a different role from the KD temperature \citep{guo2017calibration}.

EA-KD weights examples by teacher and student entropy, up-weighting high-entropy samples without a threshold; entropy does not say which class relations to trust \citep{su2025eakd}. TPKD (a 2026 preprint) projects teacher directions to preserve task margins, asking whether an update threatens the task rather than whether the teacher looks uncertain, which links it to the update rules of \S\ref{sec:kd_moo} \citep{yuan2026tpkd}.

Target revision and sample selection use different mechanisms. Label Revision and Data Selection (LR-DS) revises teacher labels with ground truth and selects a distillation subset by influence; examples outside it keep label supervision \citep{lan2025labelrevision}. RLD refines teacher logits with labels, retaining class relations when the teacher's top class is wrong, unlike CTSL's switch. Its evidence covers CIFAR-100 and ImageNet, but does not show that every retained relation helps \citep{sun2025rld}.

MCD weights the KD and CE terms by whether the teacher is correct: a correct teacher gets a large distillation weight, while for a wrong teacher the student leans on the label but still takes inter-class relations from the teacher; it needs no extra parameters and reports 76.81\% on ResNet32$\times$4$\to$ResNet8$\times$4 \citep{mishra2026mcd}.

CHAD learns sample weights from alignment between distillation and validation-loss gradients. On BanSum, teacher-confidence and surface-agreement gates stayed within 0.0001 ROUGE-L of cross-entropy; CHAD improved over standard KD by 0.0173 ROUGE-L \citep{sumit2026chad}. % VERIFIED: CHAD is a 2026 arXiv preprint; BanSum table reports the cited deltas.

Gradient alignment is a local proxy rather than a causal test, and the gains are specific to low-resource summarisation, so CHAD is background here.

KD-AIF weights samples with influence functions but also updates the teacher (to 86.56), so its 82.20 is not comparable with fixed-teacher results \citep{wu2025kdaif}. % src: wu2025kdaif T2/T3

\paragraph{Comparing reliability signals.} Label agreement and calibration do not say whether a given update helps a given student. The methods differ in whether they weight samples, switch supervision sources, revise targets or select a training subset. Their failure cases differ: a label gate inherits annotation errors, a confidence weight trusts confident errors, and a gradient proxy depends on the validation objective.

Selection can help when reliable labels can replace harmful targets; target repair can retain class relations when the teacher's top prediction is wrong; task-preserving projection addresses interference with the student objective. The papers span speech, generation, classification and summarisation, so there is no common protocol \citep{yuan2026tpkd}.
