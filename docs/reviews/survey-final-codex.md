# Codex adversarial review: SOTA KD survey

## Verdict

**Major revision.** The manuscript has a useful, unusually frank synthesis and several carefully scoped derivations, but I would not accept it as-is at TMLR: the “systematic” contribution is not reproducible from the committed search artifacts (query dates/strings, screening log and per-record dispositions are not assembled into a complete audit trail), the benchmark tables are a hand-selected subset rather than a sufficiently inspectable comparison despite strong headline range claims, and the central KD-as-MOO framing overstates what existing empirical evidence establishes. The local evidence supports most spot-checked numbers, and the authors correctly disclaim cross-paper ranking; the fixes are primarily methodological transparency, qualifications, and a few concrete wording/provenance corrections rather than a wholesale rewrite.

## The good

- The manuscript explicitly separates reported results from controlled comparisons and names stacking, changed teachers, changed baselines, and run-count variation as confounds ([08_benchmarks.tex:10-23](../../paper/survey/sections/08_benchmarks.tex)). The caveat is repeated in the abstract (PDF p. 1) and table flags (PDF pp. 15-16), which is good research practice.
- Search counts reconcile: 656 + 985 + 552 = 2,193; the three search records report 96 + 130 + 84 = 310 full-text assessments; and `docs/survey/search/README.md` describes the 445-record gap pass and final 112-method set. The flow figure is useful, and the spot-check record does disclose inaccessible cases and denominator limitations.
- The manuscript is clear about the limitation of using PCD as a reference rather than claiming it has been tested on KD ([06b_kd_moo.tex:45-50](../../paper/survey/sections/06b_kd_moo.tex)). It explicitly states the PCD source’s experiments are pruning/low-rank, not distillation.
- The DTO-KD derivation identifies an important distinction between the paper’s sum-to-one closed form and simplex-constrained MGDA, and it warns that the deployed amortized procedure is not the closed form ([06b_kd_moo.tex:20-32](../../paper/survey/sections/06b_kd_moo.tex)). This is substantive technical synthesis, not just taxonomy.
- The survey reports a counterintuitive source-level issue in SCKD’s gate condition rather than repeating the authors’ prose uncritically ([06b_kd_moo.tex:36-42](../../paper/survey/sections/06b_kd_moo.tex)). This is exactly the kind of adversarial synthesis a survey should provide.
- The strongest CIFAR-100 headline values were checked against records that identify the primary table: DeepKD 79.25 is explicitly CRLD+DeepKD, VRM is 78.76, and SKD’s baseline is flagged as 74.75. The table does not present those as a fair leaderboard.

## The bad

### Major

1. **Systematic-search reproducibility is not yet demonstrated by the committed artifact.** The PDF and `01_intro.tex:38-46` give venue groups, counts, screening totals, and criteria, but the committed search records do not constitute an auditable per-record flow ledger with exact query strings, search dates/platforms, duplicate-removal procedure, reviewer decisions, and reasons for exclusion. The search README also says the criteria were frozen in `tasks/plan.md`, which is not among the supplied committed paper materials. A reported 20-inclusion/20-exclusion spot-check does not substitute for reproducible screening. **Smallest fix:** add a compact supplementary screening appendix or machine-readable ledger containing dated queries, dedup rules, and per-record disposition/reason; link it in the paper. Until then, call this a documented structured search rather than a reproducible systematic review.
2. **Headline ImageNet range requires a visible audit trail.** The abstract says “all but seven of 79 rows lie between 71.0% and 73.0%” (PDF p. 1), while the main table shows only selected high results and the complete 79 rows live in a markdown supplement. A reader cannot validate this distribution from the main paper, and the supplement records transcribed claims rather than normalized protocols. **Smallest fix:** add the min/max and count outside the interval to the table caption or a small distribution summary, with explicit denominator/exclusions and a reference to the complete row-level source list.
3. **The MOO framing sometimes slides from an organizing lens into a factual characterization of KD.** The manuscript defines conflict in parameter space ([02_prelim.tex:64-69](../../paper/survey/sections/02_prelim.tex)) but the motivating numerical illustration is only logit-space. The paper also acknowledges that few KD papers measure conflict directly ([06b_kd_moo.tex:107-114]) and gives no common-protocol test showing conflict is frequent or causal. Thus “KD as multi-objective optimization” is a useful formulation, but not yet an empirically established explanation for SOTA gains. **Smallest fix:** state this limitation in the abstract/introduction and label the MOO taxonomy as a lens for organizing update rules; keep normalization, scale, weighting, and conflict conceptually distinct.
4. **Scope/category inconsistency weakens the taxonomy.** The inclusion rule limits the core method set to image-classification teacher-signal methods, but §V includes language-generation, speech, and summarization approaches as background and discusses them in the same “teacher reliability” taxonomy/table ([05_reliability.tex:8-28]). The section notes these domains, but the distinction between included methods and out-of-scope analogues is not immediately clear. **Smallest fix:** label the table explicitly “cross-domain background (not part of the included 112)” and visually separate it from in-scope classification methods.

### Minor

1. **Abstract says the shared benchmark protocol too broadly.** “On the shared CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K protocol” (PDF p. 1) implies stronger protocol uniformity than the paper later describes: standard pairs overlap, but teacher training, augmentations, epochs, label smoothing, run count, and baselines vary. **Smallest fix:** change to “on commonly reported CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K pairs, with protocol deviations flagged.”
2. **Bibliography date convention is not self-explanatory.** Several papers list venue-year in the citation while the registry `date` is their earlier preprint date (e.g., CTKD 2023 venue / 2022 date; GPD 2025 venue / 2024 date). This is often correct, but mixed year semantics are easy to misread. **Smallest fix:** consistently define citation year as venue year for published work and preprint year only for preprints, then spot-check all references against that rule.
3. **Supplementary markdown is a fragile journal artifact.** The manuscript says “supplementary material” but points to `included-methods.md`, a repository path. **Smallest fix:** provide that table as an actual TMLR supplement (CSV/PDF or supported appendix) and preserve the source links/table locations.
4. **Search spot-check summary needs careful wording.** `spotcheck-codex.md` records 0/20 accessible inclusion errors and 0/20 accessible exclusion errors, with two inaccessible inclusion items and ten inaccessible exclusion rows; this is appropriately surfaced in the record but the paper’s short sentence can be read as a clean 20+20 fully verified sample. **Smallest fix:** say “among accessible sampled papers” and state the inaccessible denominators, as the search README already does for 18 inaccessible full texts overall.

## Citation audit

**(a) Uncited method/results.** In the main prose and benchmark tables, the named methods/results checked have citations in their sentence or table row. The largest tables use row citations and captions identify the reference baseline. Figures are mostly taxonomy/search diagrams, and representative named leaves are not individually cited inside the figure; the adjacent paragraph/section citations provide some context but not a citation per leaf. **Smallest fix:** add citations to representative method labels in the taxonomy figure or state clearly that its labels are pointers to cited sections.

**(b) Citations used for unsupported claims.** No clear case was found in the 15+ numerical/factual spot checks above. Two claims merit tighter wording: (1) Hinton et al. supports high-temperature squared-error behavior under stated conditions, but “keeps the two terms' relative contribution roughly fixed” in `02_prelim.tex:77-79` is heuristic and depends on logits/optimization; make the approximation explicit. (2) “most of this work reports results on the same shared protocol” (`01_intro.tex:16-18`) is broader than the later caveats and should say “commonly reported pairs.” These are qualification issues, not demonstrated citation falsifications.

**(c) Bibliography metadata spot-check (12 entries).** Compared citation key/title/year/venue/author metadata in `paper/survey/refs.bib` and `literature/sources.yaml` with the registry’s stated primary-source verification notes and the corresponding search records where available: Hinton 2015; DKD 2022; CTKD 2023; LSKD 2024; DIST 2022; DTO-KD 2026; TPKD 2026; DeepKD 2025; VRM 2025; SKD 2026; PCD 2026; TopKD 2024. No material author/venue/year mismatch surfaced in this sample. Note the registry distinguishes preprint date from venue year, while BibTeX generally uses venue year for published work. This is a spot-check, not a full 66-entry bibliography audit.

**(d) Leftover notes/placeholders in PDF.** `pdftotext -layout paper/survey/build/main.pdf` (23 pages) search for TODO, TBD, FIXME, XXXX, MM, and PLACEHOLDER found no visible placeholder or internal note. The source does contain unused `\def\month{MM}` and `\def\openreview{...XXXX}` definitions in `main.tex:19-20`; they do not appear in the built PDF. Remove them before submission to avoid template residue if the source changes.

## Mock TMLR review

**Summary.** This is a broad survey of recent image-classification KD methods with a distinctive attempt to connect task/KD update rules to multi-objective optimization. It is candid about reported-number comparability and contains useful technical derivations, including a meaningful correction/qualification of published optimization rules. The central contribution is potentially publishable, but the systematic-search claim and the empirical status of the MOO framing need clearer boundaries and stronger supporting artifacts.

**Strengths.** The topic is timely; the taxonomy spans transfer signal, training scheme, teacher reliability, and objective combination; source-level caveats are unusually good; the manuscript highlights stacked results and changed settings; and the DTO-KD/PCD discussion distinguishes guarantees, deployed algorithms, and untested KD adaptations.

**Weaknesses.** Search methods and record-level screening are not reproducible enough for “systematic” as used in the title; the full leaderboard is relegated to a fragile markdown artifact and the headline distribution is not independently inspectable in the paper; the MOO lens risks implying observed conflict when the survey itself says direct measurement is scarce; and out-of-scope reliability examples blur the method-set boundary. The bibliography audit here is only a spot-check.

**Questions.** Can the authors deposit exact searches and a per-record screening/disposition ledger? How many of the 79 ImageNet rows are genuinely on the same teacher/training/augmentation protocol, and do the seven outliers remain after harmonizing definitions? What evidence demonstrates parameter-gradient conflict in ordinary KD and distinguishes conflict resolution gains from loss scaling/normalization gains? Which reliability methods belong to the 112-method set versus background analogues?

**Recommendation.** Major revision.

**Confidence.** 0.78. Confidence is high on the manuscript/source consistency issues and the cited spot checks; moderate on the overall systematic-review adequacy because the audit was limited to committed materials and the locally supplied source/search records, not every one of the 66 primary PDFs.
